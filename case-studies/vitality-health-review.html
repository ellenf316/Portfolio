Vitality Health Review Card

Turning a dense, paragraph-heavy prompt into a scannable, benefit-led card — while keeping marketing, product, and two very different audiences aligned on every word.

Company
Vitality Group
Product
Vitality Health Review
Role
Content Designer

Challenge

Vitality Group licenses its health and wellness engagement program to insurers and employers — the direct, B2B client — who in turn offer it to their own members as a benefit. Those members are Vitality's B2B2C audience: the people who actually open the app, read the copy, and decide whether to act on it.

The Vitality Health Review is the core questionnaire members complete to unlock personalized recommendations. I was asked to review the existing prompt card — the entry point members see before starting the questionnaire — and update the copy. The card needed to stay accurate to what the product actually does, satisfy what our B2B clients expected it to communicate, and be genuinely easy for a member to read and act on in a few seconds.

My Role

I worked directly with marketing and product to validate every claim in the card — what the Health Review actually asks, what "Vitality Age" means, what unlocking the wheel spin requires — and to make sure the rewritten copy worked for both audiences it serves: the B2B client whose program and brand it represents, and the individual B2B2C member who has to read it, understand it, and click through.

Before & After

Same card, same information, restructured. The before version is the version members originally saw; the after version is the rewrite.

Before

Ashleigh, complete the Vitality Health Review to get a more personalized experience.

Providing your health and lifestyle information in the Vitality Health Review will reveal your Vitality Age* and help Vitality suggest personalized activities for you. Plus, completing the VHR earns you a spin on the Vitality wheel.

This brief, confidential questionnaire will ask you questions about your health results, activity, habits, nutrition, lifestyle, mental well-being, work life and home life to better understand you.

*The Vitality Age is an overall indicator of your health risk(s) based on the information you have provided us.

I'll do this later Start Now

After

<Name>, complete the health review to get a personalized Vitality experience:

  • Learn your Vitality Age*
  • Get personalized suggested healthy activities based on your answers.
  • Earn a spin on the Vitality wheel!

The Vitality Health Review includes questions about your:

  • Health results
  • Physical activity
  • Habits
  • Nutrition
  • Lifestyle
  • Mental wellbeing

*Your Vitality Age is an overall indication of your health risks based on the information you provide.

I'll do this later Take Vitality Health Review

What Changed and Why

Led with the benefit, not just the ask

The before headline asks the member to complete the review "to get a more personalized experience" — a vague payoff attached to a request. The after headline makes the same ask, then immediately follows with a scannable list of three concrete things the member gets: their Vitality Age, personalized activity suggestions, and a wheel spin. The benefit stops being a promise and becomes a list of facts.

Gave the fun incentive equal billing

In the before version, the wheel spin — arguably the most motivating line on the card — is a bolded aside buried in the middle of a dense sentence, easy to skim past. In the after version, it's the third bullet in the benefits list, sitting on equal footing with the practical benefits instead of reading like an afterthought.

Separated scope from benefits

The before version crams the review's topic list — health results, activity, habits, nutrition, lifestyle, mental well-being, work life, and home life — into the same paragraph as the benefits, so both get harder to scan. The after version gives the topic list its own labeled section and its own bullet list, and trims it to six clear topics instead of eight.

Rewrote the CTA to say what it does

"START NOW" is generic, shouty in all caps, and doesn't name what happens when you click it. "Take Vitality Health Review" names the product and the action in one label, drops the all-caps, and removes any ambiguity about what the button does — which matters more, not less, in a B2B2C context where members are seeing this card inside their own insurer or employer's branded app, often without much other context.

Reflection

This project was less about finding new words and more about restructuring information that was already accurate but hard to use. Every fact in the before version was true; it just wasn't organized for a member to act on quickly. Working across marketing and product meant every structural change — splitting benefits from scope, cutting two topics from the list, rewriting the CTA — had to be checked against what the B2B client's program actually promised and what the underlying questionnaire actually asked, so the clearer version stayed just as accurate as the original.
